## LOCAL

[MUSIC: sting]

### 1. Governor-elect Haaland's first day

CHINO [proud]: Closer to home, the big one. Deb Haaland won the governor's race Tuesday. She beat Republican Gregg Hull, the former mayor of Rio Rancho. She's from Laguna Pueblo, and she's the first Native American woman ever elected governor of any state.

ALEX [steady]: Wednesday morning she was at Barelas Coffee House with Lieutenant Governor-elect Stephanie Garcia Richard. Her priorities: affordability, education, public safety, finding a new leader for CYFD, and protecting people's health coverage.

CHINO [curious]: Y Doc, she also said something about data centers, ¿verdad?

ALEX [explaining, engineer]: Right. On day one she wants a moratorium on large-scale data centers until the Legislature passes guardrails. Her reasons: water, land, air, and keeping power bills from going up. Speaking as an engineer, pues, the basics are simple: these buildings turn a huge amount of electricity into heat, and a lot of them cool with water. How much water depends on the design. Supporters point to jobs and tax revenue, so the real question is what the rules end up looking like.

CHINO [nodding]: Neta, in a drought, it's a fair question. She also launched HaalandTransition.com, for anyone who wants to work in her administration. She takes office in January.

### 2. The DWI scandal reaches deeper into BCSO

CHINO [serious, slower]: Ahora sí, hablando en serio. The DWI corruption scandal, otra vez.

ALEX [serious, precise]: A federal grand jury indicted former Bernalillo County sheriff's deputy Paul Jessen Junior. Prosecutors allege that from March 2018 to January 2024 he skipped court on purpose so defense attorney Thomas Clear's DWI clients could get their cases dismissed. Instead of cash, the indictment says, he took gifts: a Pit Boss grill, a big-screen TV, Craftsman tools, free legal help and hotel rooms.

CHINO [frustrated]: Qué caray. And these are drunk-driving cases, Doc. People who could hurt somebody on Coors or on I-25.

ALEX [measured]: Exactly. These are allegations. He hasn't been convicted on these charges. But earlier this year he was convicted in a separate case of obstructing a DEA investigation, and BCSO fired him in September. The indictment also mentions an unnamed senior BCSO deputy, so this story isn't over.

### Rapid-fire local

ALEX [brisk, clear]: Rapid-fire local. On your ballot: Congressman Gabe Vasquez won reelection in the Second District, per Source New Mexico.

CHINO [surprised]: Y pasó la Enmienda 4. Starting in 2029, legislators get a salary of around sixty-eight thousand dollars. Right now New Mexico is the only state that doesn't pay them at all.

ALEX [neutral]: Supporters say regular people can finally afford to serve. Critics like the Rio Grande Foundation say the number's too high. And about seventy percent of Albuquerque voters made the ACS director a chief-level position.

CHINO [concerned]: En Sandoval County, a disaster emergency after flooding washed out roads and damaged water systems, especially around the Ojo Encino, Torreon and Counselor chapters of the Navajo Nation.

ALEX [lighter]: And a good one: River of Lights opens November 28th for its 29th year, with fifty new sculptures. Tickets go on sale today at ten.

CHINO [delighted]: Y Blake's Lotaburger is the sponsor this year, Doc. Lights and a green chile cheeseburger. Bien bonito.

## UNITED STATES

[MUSIC: sting]

### 1. Democrats take Congress

ALEX [clear, analytical]: Switching gears to the national picture. Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate on Tuesday. As of Thursday morning, CNBC counted at least 250 Democratic seats in the House, and a few races are still being counted.

CHINO [impressed]: ¡Doscientos cincuenta! Está bien reborujado Washington, Doc.

ALEX [explaining]: Pues, for context: the president's party has lost House seats in almost every midterm since World War II. The only exceptions were 1998 and 2002. So the direction isn't a surprise. The size is. According to NPR, cost of living was the top issue.

CHINO [lively]: And on the Republican side, NPR reports Speaker Mike Johnson won't stay on as the top House Republican. Vice President Vance blamed the war with Iran and high gas prices. And President Trump told CNBC he can work with Democrats on "many more things than people understand."

ALEX [fair, even]: Republicans argue it was a bad cycle driven by a war and fuel prices, not a rejection of their agenda. Democrats say voters were rejecting high costs and corruption. We'll know more when the full exit data comes out.

### 2. Texas, and the data center fight

CHINO [excited]: Y aquí al ladito, en Texas: Democrat James Talarico won the Senate race against Ken Paxton, and Democrat Gina Hinojosa won governor. According to analysts at XTB, Elon Musk spent a record two hundred twenty-four million dollars backing Paxton. ¡No mames! Y aun así perdió.

ALEX [dry]: Uy, that's an expensive lesson. And Hinojosa has promised tougher rules for data centers, kind of like Haaland here. Wall Street noticed: AI stocks led a market drop Thursday, per Al Jazeera. Goldman Sachs estimates about forty-five percent of planned US data center capacity is in states that could tighten the rules, though it expects any pauses to be short.

CHINO [practical]: Okay, Doc, pero mi 401(k). ¿Me preocupo o no?

ALEX [calm]: Not financial advice, but election-week swings are normal, and historically markets have done well in the months after a midterm. The bigger story is that data centers are now a real political fight here in the Southwest.

### Rapid-fire national

CHINO [brisk]: Rapid-fire. Harmeet Dhillon, the head of the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, is out after about eighteen months.

ALEX [neutral]: NPR reports more people squeezed by prices are turning to their public libraries, and not just for books. Streaming, musical instruments, even sewing machines.

CHINO [warm]: ¡Eso, la biblioteca! And per NPR, a new report says family wealth is widening the gap between people who can buy a home and people who are priced out.

## WORLD

[MUSIC: sting-serious]

### 1. Ukraine

CHINO [serious, slower]: En otras noticias, y esta es difícil.

ALEX [somber, no humor]: In Ukraine, on Monday, Russia bombed a school in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, that was being used to hand out humanitarian aid. Ukrainian officials say a second wave of bombs hit the shelter where people were taking cover. Early reports said 24 to 26 people were killed. Reuters now reports Ukrainian police put the toll at 42.

CHINO [quiet]: Gente haciendo fila por comida. No hay palabras.

ALEX [steady]: On the diplomatic side, Turkey's President Erdogan says he wants to talk with Putin about ending the war. Italy is finalizing a new military aid package for Ukraine. And Zelensky has said talks with the US and Russia in Abu Dhabi could happen as soon as November 10th.

### 2. Iran war and Gulf shipping

CHINO [serious]: Meanwhile, in the Gulf, the war with Iran keeps hitting shipping. The British military says a tanker was struck by projectiles off the coast of Qatar this week.

ALEX [analytical]: And it ties back to Tuesday. NPR reports the new Democratic majority could pressure the White House to end the war. But Al Jazeera reports President Trump said the US may have to "destroy" the rest of Iran. So the signals are mixed.

### Rapid-fire world

ALEX [brisk, clear]: Now, around the world. In Hong Kong, NPR reports pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong was sentenced to ten years, his second conviction under the national security law. Beijing says the law restored stability. Critics say it crushes peaceful dissent.

CHINO [curious]: En Europa, bomb threats against several flights. Czech police say four planes headed to Prague were targeted. So far they've all been hoaxes, and the EU says it's too early to know if they're connected.

ALEX [lighter]: And New Zealand votes tomorrow. Polls show the two biggest parties within a couple of points of each other.

CHINO [joking, light]: Todo lo que sé de Nueva Zelanda es El Señor de los Anillos, Doc.
