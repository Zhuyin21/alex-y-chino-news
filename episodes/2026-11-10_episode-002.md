## LOCAL

### 1. Heinrich's energy bill splits New Mexico's delegation

CHINO [curious]: Closer to home. Doc, el senador Heinrich anda muy movido en Washington con su ley de energía. ¿Qué pasó ayer?

ALEX [clear, explaining]: Un paso grande. The Senate voted seventy-three to twenty-five to move Heinrich's permitting bill forward. Se llama la Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act. Es un voto de procedimiento, todavía no es el voto final. La idea es acelerar los permisos federales para líneas de transmisión, ductos y minería. And per the Journal, it would make data centers pay their own costs to connect to the power grid, so the rest of us don't get stuck with that bill.

CHINO [surprised]: Eso suena bien pa' mi recibo de la luz. But I heard not everybody in New Mexico is happy.

ALEX [measured]: Nombre, the delegation is split. El senador Ben Ray Luján votó que no. He said he can't support it in its current form. Las representantes Melanie Stansbury y Teresa Leger Fernández también se oponen. And the All Pueblo Council of Governors condemned the amended version. According to the Journal, the pueblos worry it would make uranium projects harder to challenge, including one proposed on Mount Taylor, which many pueblos consider sacred.

CHINO [thoughtful]: So what does Heinrich say?

ALEX [fair, even]: Él dice que va a bajar los recibos de la luz, y los sindicatos, como el de los electricistas, lo apoyan. The Santa Fe New Mexican reports he amended it Saturday after concerns from tribes and unions, and the Journal says he's still open to more changes. Para que tengas idea de lo que está en juego: the Journal cites a McKinsey estimate of almost forty-four billion dollars in New Mexico energy projects under federal review last year.

CHINO [nodding]: Pues son dos lados que tienen razón. Unos quieren luz más barata y empleos, y los pueblos quieren proteger su tierra sagrada. So what happens next?

ALEX [clear]: Primero, el voto final en el Senado. Then, per analysts quoted by the Journal, the House would have to agree by January third, before the new Congress starts. Si no, se cae.

### 2. Feds investigate APS and Santa Fe schools

CHINO [steady]: Y cambiando de tema, esta es de las escuelas, and a lot of parents are going to care about this one. El gobierno federal está investigando a APS y a las escuelas de Santa Fe.

ALEX [clear]: El Departamento de Educación lo anunció el lunes. El tema son las políticas que permiten que un estudiante use otro nombre y otros pronombres en la escuela sin que la escuela les avise a los papás. Federal officials say that violates parents' rights, and the Santa Fe New Mexican reports they'll look at two federal student privacy laws. Education Secretary Linda McMahon said they'll hold districts accountable, quote, "to bring parents out of the dark."

CHINO [curious]: And what are the schools saying?

ALEX [measured]: APS told KOAT its policies follow federal privacy rules and that it'll respond through the proper channels. Santa Fe says nobody has contacted it yet, and that its policies do require parental consent to change a student's name or gender in the official system.

CHINO [thoughtful]: Okay, aquí hay dos lados, y los dos quieren cuidar a los niños. Los papás dicen: es mi hijo y tengo derecho a saber. And advocates say some kids aren't safe at home, and the school might be the only place they can talk.

ALEX [steady]: Ése es el debate. Y no es nomás aquí: the Journal cites Education Week counting at least two hundred twenty-two similar federal investigations nationwide.

## UNITED STATES

[MUSIC: sting]

### 1. The House could vote on impeaching Hegseth

ALEX [clear, analytical]: Meanwhile, over in DC: the House is back, and it could vote this week on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The push comes from a Republican, Thomas Massie of Kentucky. He introduced updated articles on Monday, which starts the clock to force a vote.

CHINO [surprised]: ¡No manches, Doc! A Republican? ¿Y de qué lo acusan?

ALEX [precise]: CBS News reports eight articles. Three accuse Hegseth of waging war on Iran without authorization from Congress. Otro dice que ignoró las leyes para evitar muertes de civiles, citing a strike on an Iranian school earlier this year. Others cite the boat strikes in the Caribbean, the capture of Nicolás Maduro, and the Pentagon's scrutiny of Senator Mark Kelly.

CHINO [curious]: And what does Hegseth say?

ALEX [fair, even]: He calls them "sham articles of impeachment." Según KCRA, Hegseth says his critics' "disagreements are with success," and President Trump keeps defending him. Massie contesta que no se trata de si la guerra es popular, sino de si se violaron leyes. Democrats like Hakeem Jeffries say they're a "hard yes." Pero algunos republicanos no están convencidos. Representative French Hill said not doing a good job doesn't automatically mean an impeachable offense.

CHINO [thoughtful]: And even if it passes the House, the Senate needs two-thirds to remove him, ¿verdad?

ALEX [nodding]: Simón. Two-thirds to convict. Y ni Massie está seguro de que tiene los votos. And per KCRA, Hegseth holds his first formal press conference since May this morning.

### 2. Supreme Court lets the Big Bend border wall resume

CHINO [steady]: Switching gears, y esta es aquí nomás en West Texas. La Corte Suprema dejó que siga la construcción del muro en Big Bend, con todo y el parque nacional.

ALEX [clear]: Fue ocho a uno. La jueza Ketanji Brown Jackson votó en contra, sin dar razones. Un juez federal en El Paso, Kathleen Cardone, había parado la obra el dos de octubre. She was worried the government went too far in waiving environmental laws. The Supreme Court wrote that, quote, "the Government's interest therefore outweighs any aesthetic, recreational, and environmental harm."

CHINO [explaining]: Y ojo, que es temporal. Se queda así mientras la Corte decide si toma el caso o mientras se resuelve en El Paso. La demanda la pusieron grupos ambientalistas y vecinos de la zona. And the project includes vehicle barriers, patrol roads, cameras and sensors.

ALEX [measured]: El gobierno dice que la seguridad de la frontera es un interés urgente. The other side, per KFOX in El Paso: Bob Krumenaker of Keep Big Bend Wild warned the destruction "is likely to resume at any moment."

CHINO [nodding]: Pues pa' los que conocemos la frontera, Big Bend es de los lugares más bonitos. Security or nature: that fight isn't over yet.

### 2. Paraguay's ousted president charged after a bank collapse

ALEX [clear]: Now, around the world, bajamos a Sudamérica. Paraguay's former president, Santiago Peña, has been charged after a bank collapse.

CHINO [curious]: Esta está bien canija, Doc. What bank was it?

ALEX [explaining]: Ueno Bank, un banco digital con como tres millones de clientes. That's about half the country. El banco central lo intervino porque ya no era solvente, y la gente se quedó sin acceso a su dinero. Peña era accionista de la empresa dueña del banco. Prosecutors allege he steered public money into it, and lawmakers say he benefited personally. He tried to resign, but the Senate refused and removed him unanimously on Friday.

CHINO [surprised]: ¡Qué caray! So where is he now?

ALEX [measured]: Prosecutors charged him with breach of trust, and a judge ordered him held at a military facility before trial. Al Jazeera reports the indictment says he got about half a million dollars in loans from the bank to build his mansion. Peña niega todo. Su vicepresidente, Pedro Alliana, ya tomó protesta y va a terminar el período, hasta agosto de 2028.

CHINO [thoughtful]: Imagine waking up and your bank is just gone, Doc. Al Jazeera dice que el gobierno ya le regresó su dinero a como un millón doscientos mil clientes. Ojalá le regresen a todos.
