### Pregúntale a Doc

ASHLEY [reading, curious]: Okay, Pregúntale a Doc. This question came in on GitHub, and it's been waiting since the very first Saturday: "¿Cómo funciona la computación cuántica? ¿Qué es eso de los qubits? ¿Y cuándo podré comprar una?"

ASHLEY [nodding]: Okay, te sigo.

ALEX [explaining]: A qubit is a quantum bit. Per NIST, the National Institute of Standards and Technology, a qubit can be zero, one, or a mix of both at the same time. That's called superposition. ASHLEY [curious]: And why is that powerful?

ALEX [clear]: Because it multiplies. Two regular bits hold one combination at a time. Two qubits can hold a superposition of all four combinations, and every qubit you add doubles that. ASHLEY [excited]: So it's like trying every answer at once!

ALEX [gently correcting]: Ah, esa es la confusión más común. NIST says no, it can't just brute-force every possible answer. Cuando mides, sacas muy poquita información, so the whole trick is designing the calculation so the useful answer is what you read at the end.
