## LOCAL

[MUSIC: sting]

### 1. Heinrich's energy bill splits New Mexico's delegation

CHINO [curious]: Closer to home. Doc, el senador Heinrich anda muy movido en Washington con su ley de energía. ¿Qué pasó ayer?

ALEX [clear, explaining]: Un paso grande. The Senate voted seventy-three to twenty-five to move Heinrich's permitting bill forward. Se llama la Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act. Es un voto de procedimiento, todavía no es el voto final. La idea es acelerar los permisos federales para líneas de transmisión, ductos y minería. And per the Journal, it would make data centers pay their own costs to connect to the power grid, so the rest of us don't get stuck with that bill.

CHINO [surprised]: Eso suena bien pa' mi recibo de la luz. But I heard not everybody in New Mexico is happy.

ALEX [measured]: Nombre, the delegation is split. El senador Ben Ray Luján votó que no. He said he can't support it in its current form. Las representantes Melanie Stansbury y Teresa Leger Fernández también se oponen. And the All Pueblo Council of Governors condemned the amended version. According to the Journal, the pueblos worry it would make uranium projects harder to challenge, including one proposed on Mount Taylor, which many pueblos consider sacred.

CHINO [thoughtful]: So what does Heinrich say?

ALEX [fair, even]: Él dice que va a bajar los recibos de la luz, y los sindicatos, como el de los electricistas, lo apoyan. The Santa Fe New Mexican reports he amended it Saturday after concerns from tribes and unions, and the Journal says he's still open to more changes. Para que tengas idea de lo que está en juego: the Journal cites a McKinsey estimate of almost forty-four billion dollars in New Mexico energy projects under federal review last year.

CHINO [nodding]: Pues son dos lados que tienen razón. Unos quieren luz más barata y empleos, y los pueblos quieren proteger su tierra sagrada. So what happens next?

ALEX [clear]: Primero, el voto final en el Senado. Then, per analysts quoted by the Journal, the House would have to agree by January third, before the new Congress starts. Si no, se cae.
